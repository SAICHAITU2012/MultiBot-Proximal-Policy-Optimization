## ROOT CAUSE FOUND: the push env has been broken since Nov 6 20:48 — M0/M0b trained on garbage (2026-11-08)

The v2 audit's behavior columns (every episode dying in ~6 steps, zero crate contact for BOTH checkpoints) triggered a deeper isolation. **THE PUSH ENV ITSELF has been un-trainable since the Nov 6 20:48 `_reset_idx` rewrite.** Two independent defects, both verified by controlled experiment:

1. **THE BUFFER WIPE (the freeze):** `step()` calls `_reset_idx(self.reset_buf)` every step; with nothing terminated the mask is empty, but the rewrite's code still ran its reset path — which ends by ZEROING the env's obs/action buffers (`last_actions`, `actions`, `dof_vel`, `base_lin_vel`, `base_ang_vel`) — and then `step()` calls `_update_observation()`. Two consequences, both measured: (a) with `simulate_action_latency=True`, `step()` executes `last_actions` as joint targets — always the zeroed buffer, i.e. **the executed target was default_dof_pos every step: the policy's actions were NEVER executed (a statue)**; (b) the policy's observations showed zero dof_vel/actions/base motion — **blind**. Measured: the walker's buffer velocity reads EXACTLY 0.0000 m/s at every sample while its legs cycle (dof divergence vs a go2_env twin grows 0.10→0.57 rad); the dog treadmills and sags to belly height (z 0.30→0.26) and can never approach the crate. The SAME walker walks 0.6 m/s in go2_env with bit-identical 45-dim obs, identical actions, identical gains (kp 20/kd 0.5), identical solver options, identical geom/link build (27 geoms, 13 links, same per-link ownership). Skipping ONLY the empty-mask reset restores the approach (dog-crate 1.00→0.84 m and shrinking). NOTE (corrected by the regression-test negative control): the ROBOT's own velocity state is NOT directly wiped — the freeze is entirely the buffer wipe acting through the action-latency execution path and the observations. The M0 "lying optimum" is reinterpreted: **the optimal policy in an env where actions are never executed and observations show no motion is to output the equilibrium stance — the paws can never generate traction.**
2. **THE Z=0 SPAWN (the toss-loop):** the rewrite spawned the dog at base z=0.0 (`torch.zeros((n,1))`) instead of the upstream standing height (go2_env's `init_qpos` uses init_base_pos z=0.42). ## THE FALL-SLAM EXPLOIT (2026-11-08, found by the r1 fresh-seed sweep)

The r1 sweep (fresh seeds, 16 envs) looked excellent by the old metric — model_400: 16/16 moved, 15/16 "pushed" ≥0.15 m, mean +0.222 m — but the mission-relevant instrumentation exposed a fundamentally different reality:

1. **The old fall metric is blind in this env.** `fall_steps (z<0.15)` counted ONE fall episode for model_400 — but the TRUE termination count is **286 in 9600 env-steps**. The auto-reset respawns the fallen dog the SAME step it terminates, so a fallen dog never accumulates z<0.15 steps. The sweep metric measured sub-0.15 *dips among surviving steps* (pushing postures), not falls.
2. **All 286 terminations are real falls** (214 pitch + 74 roll; **0 solver errors** — the NaN disease did not return). Life lengths: mean 31 steps, **median 18** (0.4 s), 167/286 lives die within 20 steps.
3. **The mechanism: a fall-slam exploit.** A dog cannot reach the crate (0.8 m of approach) in an 18-step life at command speed — yet the crate accumulates displacement. The only consistent mechanism: the policy SPRINTS at the crate, TRIPS on its 15 cm face, and the fall's body-slam shoves the crate; the auto-reset respawns the dog 1.0 m behind it; repeat. The reward economics permit it: `push_progress` pays progress_scale(40) × scale(3.0) = **120 per meter**, while `fall_penalty` costs only **4 per termination** — break-even at a 3.3 cm slam. Any slam ≥3.3 cm is net-positive. This is the same economics class as the advisor's original fall-structure concern, now with clean evidence: the historical "the dog shoves it even while falling" observations were this exploit. Training degradation after iter 400 (model_1350: 2/16) is the exploit over-optimizing and collapsing.
4. **The repaired env itself is healthy** (0 solver errors, actions execute, spawn verified) — the exploit is a REWARD-ECONOMICS problem, not an env bug.

## M7 CLOSED — the delivery gap was a CONTROL LAYER, not the policy (2026-11-12)

**Result: delivered 2/2.** pkg0 `drop_distance 0.549` at t=536 s, pkg1 `drop_distance 0.547` at t=757 s, both `mode: learned`, recon 2/2, picked 1, makespan 1200 s (`logs/mission_20261112_134116`). The acceptance gate is unchanged — the real crate is inside the real 0.55 m zone.

The previous M7 hypothesis ("the policy never learned fine placement; a terminal-placement training stage is required") was **wrong**, and the evidence had been pointing at the answer all along: the learned pusher *never failed to move the crate*. It hauls 0.35-0.91 m in a single 2-6 s stroke. What was missing was the closed loop around a skill that works.

### The primitives actually available (measured, `experiments/probe_cmd_modes.py`)

| command (2 s, mission scene) | measured effect |
|---|---|
| `vx` 0.06 / 0.10 / 0.15 | 0.005 / 0.006 / 0.008 m/s — **a deadband, not a slow speed** |
| `vx` 0.20 / 0.25 / 0.30 / 0.40 | 0.078 / 0.164 / 0.268 / 0.453 m/s |
| `vx` 0.60 (2 s) | crate moves **0.289 m** — the only push speed that works |
| `vx` -0.30 | dog backs 0.456 m (reverse works; the code comment claiming it is dead was wrong) |
| `vy` ±0.4 | dog strafes 0.41-0.59 m (the most effective free motion) |
| `wz` 0.15/0.25/0.35/0.50/0.80/1.00 | 0.24/0.02/0.13/0.14/0.43/0.74 rad/s — nonlinear, near-dead in the middle |

So: translation needs a **0.30 m/s floor** (every historical "the dog froze" report is this deadband), yaw needs **bang-bang** magnitude, and the only load-moving primitive is the learned pusher at speed.

### The design that works: two phases

`retreat` → get a clear run-up behind the crate on the crate→zone line (±0.30 m) → `charge` → apply the learned pusher **continuously** with abort guards → `retreat` → repeat.

A charge is a *haul*, not a nudge: in contact the crate moves ~0.145 m/s, so 6 s of unbroken contact is ~0.8 m for free. Two strokes in the successful run were 0.836 m and 0.908 m.

The abort guards are the part the historical open-loop code never had:
- **stall detector** (no 0.015 m of crate travel in 0.9 s) — without it the dog drives *through/over* the crate, pushing it in whatever direction the drift left it;
- **climb guard** (base z > 0.40 m for > 0.25 s) — the crate is 15 cm tall; a raised base means the dog is riding up it, not pushing;
- **overrun limit** (`along > -0.10`) and a 0.90 m travel cap.

### Failure modes found by measurement (each was a real, hours-long mission stall)

| symptom | actual cause | fix |
|---|---|---|
| align/face/settle flip-flop every 0.2 s | single tolerance sat on the noise floor (standing yaw jitter ±0.05 rad) | hysteresis: enter 0.40, exit 0.25 rad |
| 190 s parked, 1 cm outside the acceptance band | the goto's arrival tolerance (0.08) was wider than the band edge (0.72) → "arrived" with no command | tolerances must nest: arrival 0.05 inside a 0.82 m band |
| 200 s creeping, crate shoved 0.19 m *away* | steering away from contact swept the body sideways into the crate | **pure axial back-off** — the only motion near a 15 cm crate that cannot nudge it |
| 30 s motionless in align | two-branch radial/orbit rule split at 0.95 m with a 0.98 m ring → 3 cm dead zone | one monotone bearing-stepping rule |
| route target jumped across the crate, dog orbited for hundreds of s | swing point chosen by the sign of a dot product, recomputed per tick | path-clearance test (`_seg_dist` < 0.60 m → orbit) |
